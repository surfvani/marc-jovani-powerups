### A.2 Per-Framework Operational Expansion (high-value entries)

**M.A.G.I.C. (Hormozi, $100M Leads, 2023, p. 75–110)** — *Magnet* (target avatar by name), *Avatar* (specific person), *Goal* (one narrow result), *Interval* (timeframe), *Container* (format word: "guide," "checklist," "playbook"). Concrete formula: "[Magnet] for [Avatar] to [Goal] in [Interval] (without [Pain])". Example for cinematic music: "The 7-Day Cinematic Score Sketch Method for Self-Taught Composers (Without a Music Degree)."

**Schwartz Awareness operationalized for titles** —
- *Unaware*: lead with **story** or **counter-intuitive observation**. Title shape: "Why [established belief] is killing your [outcome]" or pure narrative ("They laughed when I sat down at the piano…" — Caples 1925).
- *Problem-Aware*: name the pain explicitly. "How to stop [pain] without [common workaround]."
- *Solution-Aware*: differentiate **mechanism**. "The [unique mechanism] approach to [outcome]."
- *Product-Aware*: contrast features/proof. "Why [Product] beats [Alt] for [job]."
- *Most-Aware*: **offer/identity**. "Get [specific deliverable] now — [scarcity element]."

**Schwartz Sophistication operationalized** —
- *Stage 1*: simple direct claim ("Lose 10 pounds").
- *Stage 2*: bigger/superior claim ("Lose 10 pounds in 14 days").
- *Stage 3*: introduce a **named mechanism** ("The CICO Reset method…").
- *Stage 4*: **better mechanism** vs. competitors ("Why CICO fails — the Metabolic Window protocol").
- *Stage 5*: **identity / experience** ("For composers who already understand harmony but feel stuck"). Source: *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966), ch. 4.

**4Us Concrete Rating Rubric** — Score every candidate 1–4 on Useful, Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific. Reject if total <12 or any single dimension <3 (Masterson, AWAI). Operational checklist for the Hook Creator's QC pass.

**Caples 35 (Tested Advertising Methods, 5th ed., 1998, ch. 5)** — Generative starters: *How to / How / Why / Which / Who else wants / Wanted / This / Because / If / Advice to* + announcement words (Introducing, Announcing, At last, Finally) + price/offer headlines + commands (Eat that frog) + testimonials + question. Caples documented that long headlines that say something outsell short headlines that say nothing (rule 5 of Hahn's preface).

**Ogilvy 11 (Confessions, 1963, ch. 5)** — (1) What > how, (2) Built around a Big Idea, (3) Give the facts, (4) Cannot bore into buying, (5) Be well-mannered, (6) Be contemporary, (7) Committees criticize, individuals write, (8) Repeat winning ads until they stop pulling, (9) Don't write what you wouldn't show your family, (10) Image and brand, (11) Don't be a copy-cat. Verified from Wikipedia entry on book and Goodreads ToC.

**Sugarman 30 Triggers (Adweek Copywriting Handbook, 2007)** — Including: involvement/ownership, honesty, integrity, credibility, value-comparison, sense-of-urgency, exclusivity, simplicity, mental-engagement, guilt, specificity, familiarity, hope, curiosity, satisfaction-conviction, linking-current-knowledge, consistency. *Triggers* (1999) book extends to 30 numbered triggers.

**Halo Strategy (Sabri Suby, Sell Like Crazy, 2019)** — 9 questions to define dream buyer + research watering holes (Reddit, Amazon top-25 reviews, Quora, AnswerThePublic). Output: avatar + verbatim phrases for hook elements. This is the avatar-mining backbone of the Hook Creator.

**Brunson Epiphany Bridge / 3 False-Belief Patterns (Expert Secrets, ch. 9)** — For any Idea, identify three false beliefs the avatar holds: (1) Vehicle ("this approach won't work"), (2) Internal ("I can't do it"), (3) External ("circumstances prevent me"). Each becomes a candidate hook angle. Title formula: "How [normal person] [achieved outcome] despite [false belief]".

**STEPPS (Berger, Contagious, 2013)** — Social currency, Triggers, Emotion (high-arousal: awe, anger, anxiety per Berger & Milkman 2012, *J. Marketing Research* 49.2), Public, Practical Value, Stories. STEPPS is **shareability** layer, not a syntactic shape.

**MrBeast IMPACT (leaked playbook, Aug 2024, "How to Succeed in MrBeast Production," 36 pages)** — CTR is the dominant lever; "Crazy progression" structure; "I Spent 50 Hours In Ketchup" beats "I Spent 50 Hours In My Front Yard"; substitute extreme verbs ("I Survived" > "I Spent"). The leaked doc explicitly states extremity + specificity + curiosity-gap drive CTR.

**Ship 30 for 30 Headline 5 (Cole/Bush)** — From ship30for30.com guide: 5 elements = (1) Specific number, (2) Adjective/qualifier, (3) Keyword/niche term, (4) Promised outcome, (5) Time-frame or proof. Example: "5 Counter-intuitive Cinematic Composition Habits That Will Triple Your Cue Quality in 30 Days."

**Welsh 3-Line Trailer (justinwelsh.me)** — (1) Scroll-stopper (relatable enemy, throw rocks), (2) Build/flip (hero gaining ground), (3) Hook line ending in colon, ellipsis, or question that promises payoff. Verified across multiple Welsh newsletter posts including "Anatomy of a Viral LinkedIn Post" (2022).

**Quantitative anchors (cite-worthy):**
- BuzzSumo's analysis of 100M headlines (2017 by Steve Rayson; updated 2021 by Linehan, BuzzSumo) found ideal length **11 words / 65 characters** for Facebook, top trigrams "will make you," "this is why," "X reasons why," "X things you," "this is what."
- Backlinko/BuzzSumo 912M-post study (Brian Dean, 2019) — long headlines (14–17 words) outperform short by 76.7% on shares; question headlines +23.3% shares.
- Outbrain study (~65,000 paid headlines, 2014) — negative superlatives ("never," "worst") outperform positive ("always," "best") by **30%** vs control; CTR with negatives **63% higher** than positive counterparts.
- Conductor study (Nathan Safran, 2013) — Number headlines preferred most by ~36% of readers; second was "reader-addressing"; readers prefer 0–1 superlatives or 4 (the middle is dead).
- HubSpot/Outbrain 3M paid-link study (2013) — headlines containing "How to" or "amazing" hurt CTR by up to 59% in paid contexts (i.e., context-dependent — different from organic shareability).
- *Reading Dies in Complexity* (Sci. Adv., 2024; ~30K Washington Post + Upworthy A/B tests) — simpler headlines win across nearly 30,000 field experiments. Operational rule: write at ~7th-grade reading level (Caples).

|---------|-----------|------|

**2024–2026 Recency check:** BuzzSumo's 2021 100M re-analysis confirmed instructional dominates Facebook, curiosity dominates Twitter. The Sci. Adv. 2024 simpler-writing finding (~30K WaPo + Upworthy A/B tests) is the most current large-scale evidence — readers select simpler headlines in competitive feeds.

**Upworthy Research Archive (Matias et al., *Nature Scientific Data*, 2021)** — 32,487 experiments, 538M assignments, publicly released. Editors wrote 25 headlines per article; descriptive titles tended to beat clever puns; numbers + audience-referencing reliably won. June 2024 update flagged ~22% randomization issues (avoid 2013-06-25 to 2014-01-10 window for confirmatory research).

**MrBeast leaked playbook (36-page PDF, leaked August 2024 by Rosanna Pansino)** — three core metrics: CTR, AVD (Average View Duration), AVP (Average View Percentage). First-minute retention is decisive; thumbnails and titles set "expectations"; substitute extreme/concrete words for vague/general ones.

## D. AVATAR ENGINEERING AT DEPTH

### D.1 Schwartz 5 Awareness Levels — Title shape per level (worked across niches)

| Level | Signal | Cinematic Music Composition | Business / SaaS | Fitness |
|-------|--------|------------------------------|-----------------|---------|
| **Unaware** | Not searching for anything related; no vocabulary | "Why your favorite movie scenes feel like that" (story/observation) | "Why your team is busy but unproductive" | "The reason your mood crashes at 3pm" |
| **Problem-Aware** | Names the pain; doesn't know solution category | "How to stop your scores sounding like demos" | "How to escape constant context-switching" | "Why diet & cardio aren't working" |
| **Solution-Aware** | Knows category; comparing approaches | "The orchestral mockup approach for solo composers" | "Async-first work for distributed teams" | "Resistance training over cardio for fat loss" |
| **Product-Aware** | Knows providers; needs differentiation | "Cinematic Composing's Hybrid Scoring Method vs. Spitfire courses" | "Linear vs. Notion for solo founders" | "5/3/1 vs. nSuns for intermediate lifters" |
| **Most-Aware** | Ready to buy; needs offer/scarcity | "Hybrid Scoring 4.0 — enrollment closes Friday" | "Notion Pro upgrade — 30% off until Sunday" | "5/3/1 12-week template — last 50 spots" |

### D.2 Schwartz Sophistication — How title shape changes

- **Stage 1 (1st-to-market):** Simple promise. "Compose cinematic music in your DAW."
- **Stage 2 (more claims):** Bigger/faster. "Compose pro-level cinematic cues in 30 days."
- **Stage 3 (mechanism):** Named system. "The 7-Layer Hybrid Method for cinematic composition."
- **Stage 4 (better mechanism):** Counter-claim. "Why orchestration templates fail — the Hybrid Layer-Stack approach."
- **Stage 5 (identity/experience):** Tribe + creator. "For self-taught composers who already understand harmony but feel stuck on emotional arc."

### D.3 "Conversation in their head" — Operationalized

Robert Collier (1931): "Always enter the conversation already taking place in the customer's mind." Operational steps:
1. **Halbert/Schwartz mass-desire detection** — what is the avatar already lying awake about?
2. **Sales Safari (Hoy & Hillman)** — observe forums; categorize quotes into Pain / Jargon / Worldview / Recommendations quadrants.
3. **Joanna Wiebe review-mining** — clip verbatim phrases (4-star Amazon reviews are richest); use the customer's exact words as title elements.
4. **Suby Halo Strategy** — Reddit + Amazon top-25 reviews + AnswerThePublic + YouTube comments; aggregate into 9-question buyer-persona document (Sell Like Crazy, 2019).
5. **Welsh's enemy-throw** — name the relatable enemy the avatar already blames.

### D.4 Avatar/Persona engineering — Best primary sources

- **JTBD–Christensen path:** *The Innovator's Solution* (2003); *Competing Against Luck* (2016) — "milkshake" interview.
- **JTBD–Ulwick path:** *What Customers Want* (2005); *Jobs to be Done: Theory to Practice* (2016) — outcome-driven, 84-step ODI process.
- **Demand-Side path:** Bob Moesta, *Demand-Side Sales 101* (2020) — Push/Pull, Anxiety/Habit "4 Forces."
- **Empathy Map:** XPLANE's canonical 6-quadrant template (Says/Thinks/Does/Feels + Pains/Gains).
- **Halo Strategy avatar deep-dive:** Suby, *Sell Like Crazy* (2019), Phase 1.
- **StoryBrand BrandScript:** Miller (2017) — Character-Problem-Guide-Plan-CTA-Failure-Success worksheet.
- **Dream 100:** Brunson, *Traffic Secrets* (2020); originally Chet Holmes, *Ultimate Sales Machine* (2007).

### D.5 Voice-of-customer / forum-mining toolkit (2024–2026)

- **Reddit:** Native API (post-2023 changes); PullPush.io (Pushshift successor); Reveddit (deleted comments); Camas Reddit search; GummySearch (paid, AI-powered subreddit insights); SubredditStats.com.
- **Amazon review mining:** Helium 10 (paid); ReviewMeta; AMZScout. Wiebe's manual method: clip from 4-star reviews using a Notion or Google Doc with Before-state / Dream-state columns.
- **AnswerThePublic alternatives:** AlsoAsked.com; Keyword Insights; Glimpse.
- **SparkToro alternatives:** Audiense; HypeAuditor.
- **Surveys:** Typeform, Tally.so, Riff (newer 2024).
- **Sethi/Suby 5-question survey** (verbatim, from Suby's *Sell Like Crazy* and Sethi's *Earnable* materials):
 1. What's your biggest single challenge with [topic]?
 2. What have you tried that didn't work?
 3. If you could wave a magic wand and have anything related to [topic], what would it be?
 4. What's stopping you from solving this right now?
 5. In your own words, describe a perfect outcome.

### D.6 Review-mining → title-pivot pipeline

1. **Mine** verbatim phrases from Amazon reviews, Reddit, YouTube comments (Wiebe Copyhackers method).
2. **Tag** by Pain / Jargon / Worldview / Recommendation (Hoy/Hillman Sales Safari quadrants).
3. **Cluster** repeated phrases (frequency proxy for resonance).
4. **Slot** verbatim phrases into Pattern templates (Caples, Ship 30, Welsh, MAGIC).
5. **Score** with 4Us rubric (1–4 each, reject <12 total).

Operators who codified this: Joanna Wiebe (Copyhackers, 2014–present); Amy Hoy & Alex Hillman (Stacking the Bricks, 30x500); Jay Acunzo; Justin Jackson; Ramit Sethi ("Mind Reading"); Jennifer Havice (CXL).

## E. COGNITIVE SCIENCE BACKBONE (operationalized)

| 1 | **Information-gap theory** | Loewenstein, *Psych. Bulletin* 116(1), 75–98 (1994); extended Golman & Loewenstein 2018 | Title must open a small, attractive knowledge gap — not a chasm |
| 2 | **Processing fluency** | Alter & Oppenheimer, *Personality & Soc. Psych. Review* 13 (2009) — "Uniting the Tribes of Fluency" | Simpler/easier titles judged more true, more liked. Confirmed by Sci. Adv. 2024 simpler-writing study |
| 3 | **Berger–Milkman virality** | *J. Marketing Research* 49(2), 192–205 (2012) | High-arousal emotion (awe, anger, anxiety) > low-arousal (sadness). Positive valence helps but arousal dominates |
| 4 | **Peak-end rule** | Kahneman & Redelmeier (1996) | Title is the peak; ensure ending word is concrete and emotionally loaded |
| 5 | **Von Restorff (isolation)** | Von Restorff (1933); modern replications in eye-tracking (Hunt 1995) | Pattern-break a single word (coined word, ALL-CAPS, parenthetical) |
| 6 | **Specificity / sharp numbers** | Schindler & Yalch, *Adv. Consumer Research* 33, 586–590 (2006) — 47% rated ~10% more accurate than 50% | Use sharp numbers ("47%" not "50%") |
| 7 | **Negativity bias** | Outbrain 2014; Robertson et al., *Nature Human Behaviour* 7 (2023) — negativity drives clicks on news headlines | Negative superlatives + 30% CTR; one negative word per title max |
| 8 | **Self-reference effect** | Symons & Johnson, *Psychological Bulletin* 121 (1997) — meta-analysis | "You/your" outperforms third-person; BuzzSumo confirms |
| 9 | **Identity signaling** | Berger & Heath, *J. Consumer Research* 34 (2008) — "Identity Signaling…" | Title names the tribe ("for self-taught composers") |
| 10 | **Forer/Barnum** | Forer, *J. Abnormal Soc. Psych.* 44 (1949) | High-resonance vague statements feel personally targeted |
| 11 | **Gestalt closure** | Wertheimer (1923) | Incomplete title sets up reader to "complete" by clicking |
| 12 | **Endowed progress** | Nunes & Drèze, *J. Consumer Research* 32 (2006) | Numbered lists imply progress; "30 ways" beats "ways" by 15% (Moz) |
| 13 | **Picture-superiority / dual coding** | Paivio (1971; 1991) | Concrete imagery words ("ketchup" not "yard") encode better |
| 14 | **Predictive coding / free energy** | Friston (2010, *Nat. Rev. Neurosci.* 11) | Curiosity = prediction error; the title should violate one expected pattern |
| 15 | **System 1 / System 2** | Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011) | Title processed by System 1 in <1s — must be fluent + emotional. Don't ask System 2 to evaluate |

**Operational Lessons Encoded for the Hook Creator:**
1. *Test, don't guess* — reserve a "candidate-pool generator" mode that produces 25+ variants (Upworthy rule) and a separate "score" mode (4Us, MAGIC, IMPACT).
2. *Coin or borrow a single distinctive word.*
3. *Promise + subtitle structure* for books; single decisive line for headlines.
4. *Substitute concrete for general* (Ketchup not Yard).
5. *Sharp numbers > round numbers* (Schindler & Yalch).
6. *Test against cold traffic* (AdWords, polls, sponsored shares).
7. *Title should encode at least one of: curiosity gap, identity marker, sharp number, named mechanism, or counter-conventional claim.*

### G.3 Decision rules (operational pseudocode for the skill)

```
1. Detect Avatar Awareness Level (Unaware → Most-Aware)
2. Detect Sophistication Level (Stage 1 → Stage 5)
3. Classify Idea Type (Mechanism / Process / Identity / Big Idea / Curated / Story)
4. Map to Pattern Set (G.1 matrix)
5. Generate 25 candidates using selected Patterns

```

### CAVEATS

- **The leaked MrBeast playbook (Aug 2024) is unverified** as official by MrBeast's company; it is consistent with his public statements and was confirmed by multiple ex-employees including Rosanna Pansino. Treat its specific numbers (e.g., "21M lost in first minute of 60M") as illustrative.
- **Outbrain's "+30% / +63%" superlatives study** is a single 65,000-headline corpus from 2014 in paid-discovery context; it may not generalize equally to organic Facebook, Substack, or YouTube in 2025.
- **BuzzSumo's "ideal length 11 words / 65 chars"** is a Facebook-shares optimum; for books, longer descriptive subtitles often win; for YouTube, 40–60 character titles dominate per VidIQ/TubeBuddy 2024 data.
- **The Upworthy archive** has known randomization issues for tests run between June 2013 and January 2014 (Matias et al., June 2024 update) — exclude that window for confirmatory analysis.
- **JTBD** has at least three competing definitions (Christensen progress / Ulwick outcomes / Moesta forces). The Hook Creator should pick one (recommend Ulwick's outcome statements for title generation, as they map to "ultra-specific" 4Us).
- **All cited bestseller copy figures** are publisher-reported; sales numbers fluctuate.
- **The Heath SUCCESs framework** dates to 2007; the 2017 sticker-edition is a re-issue, not a revision.
- **Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising*** is the 1966 original; the Brian Kurtz reprint (Bottom Line Books, 2017) is the canonical modern edition.
- **"Frank Kern Results in Advance"** is documented across his Mass Control / Convert trainings and podcast; there is no single primary book — cite Kern's blog/podcast (frankkernpodcast.com) and warrior-forum threads as supporting sources.
- **Hormozi's titling discipline** is documented in his $100M Leads book (2023) and Acquisition.com YouTube videos; he explicitly endorses A/B testing names and using polls.
- **Recency bar** — Where claims rest on classic primary sources (Caples 1932, Hopkins 1923, Collier 1931, Schwartz 1966, Cialdini 1984, Loewenstein 1994), they are flagged as foundational; modern replications cited where available (Berger & Milkman 2012 for emotion-virality; Schindler & Yalch 2006 for specificity; Symons & Johnson 1997 for self-reference; Robertson et al. 2023 for negativity in news; Sci. Adv. 2024 for simpler-headline preference).
