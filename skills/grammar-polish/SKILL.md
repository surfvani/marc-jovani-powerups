# Manuscript Polish — Two-Pass Edit


You are editing a handwritten manuscript. The author's voice is casual, conversational, and honest. Your job is to clean it up without making it sound like AI wrote it.


## Pass 1: Grammar & Spelling Only


Fix ONLY:
- Spelling mistakes
- Grammar errors (subject-verb agreement, possessive vs contraction, singular/plural)
- Duplicate words ("been been")
- Broken markdown formatting (mismatched bold/italic asterisks)


Do NOT change:
- Sentence structure
- Word choice (unless the word is misspelled)
- Tone or style
- Punctuation choices that reflect the author's voice (ellipses, dashes, etc.)


Use surgical, minimal edits. One fix per edit.


After applying fixes, LIST any non-grammar issues you noticed (clarity, ambiguity, awkward phrasing, formatting) as **suggestions only** — do not modify these in the document. Present each suggestion with the line number and a short explanation of what could improve and why.


Tell the author:


"Do you want me to do a pass on Clarity? I will not change tone, I'll just focus on:


1. Improving awkward phrasing
2. Making it easier to read
3. Making it slightly more tight, clear, honest, casual, easy to understand"


## Pass 2: Clarity (if author approved)


Suggest the author do this second pass.


**Goal:** Make it easier to read on the first pass. Tight, clear, honest, casual. Plain English. Easy to understand.


**What to fix:**
- Nested clauses that force re-reading (e.g., "movies that composers that have taught at X have scored" — too many "that" and "have" stacked)
- Mixed verb forms in parallel structures (e.g., "to absorb... and then testing" — pick one: "to absorb... and then test")
- Dangling or ambiguous phrases (e.g., "from my own studio hours and finding solutions" — unclear what "finding solutions" attaches to)
- Parenthetical interruptions that break the sentence's momentum — remove or integrate
- Buried subjects or delayed main point (e.g., "in this book there are many X" → "this book is full of X")
- Unnecessary words that add no meaning


**Rules:**
- Propose each change with a short explanation before applying
- Rewrite the minimum — move a clause, swap a word, add a comma. Don't rewrite whole paragraphs.
- Never add ideas, opinions, or information the author didn't write
- Preserve the author's register: casual, direct, first-person. If the original sounds like someone talking to a friend, the edit should too.
- Don't make it "proper" — make it clear
